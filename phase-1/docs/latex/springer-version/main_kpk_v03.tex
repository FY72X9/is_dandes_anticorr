\begin{abstract}
Indonesia's \textit{Dana Desa} (Village Fund) channels Rp 71 trillion annually to 75,259 villages, yet legal prosecutions reveal systemic financial diversion lagging two to five years behind disbursement windows. Existing monitoring relies on retrospective manual audits or supervised machine learning requiring non-existent ground-truth fraud labels in real-time administrative \textit{Siskeudes} records. Grounded in Design Science Research (DSR), Agency Theory, and Fraud Diamond, this study constructs an unsupervised Dual-Path Consensus framework (\textbf{Protocol 2}) combining Isolation Forest (IF), Local Outlier Factor (LOF), and an 8-layer Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder (RDA) with an Operational Policy Mapping Layer on 96,778 activity records across 1,363 villages (2023--2025) in Jambi Province. Compared to baseline majority voting (\textbf{Protocol 1}), \textbf{Protocol 2} decouples local density isolates (LOF) from multi-model global convergence (IF $\cap$ RDA), expanding anomaly recall from 3,107 (3.12\%) to \textbf{7,153 consensus anomalous activities (7.39\%)} representing \textbf{Rp 642.85 billion in financial realization exposure (14.89\%)} and \textbf{Rp 6.08 trillion in budget ceiling exposure (7.46\%)}. Operational policy mapping reveals a major typology shift toward \textbf{T2: Ghost Activities (58.1\% / 4,155 records)} and \textbf{T5: Procurement Irregularities (32.8\% / 2,343 records)}. Longitudinal persistence identifies \textbf{702 villages (51.5\%)} flagged across all three fiscal years. On an ex-ante synthetic benchmark, \textbf{Protocol 2} achieves superior recovery (\textbf{Precision = 0.846, Recall = 0.846, F1 = 0.846, AUC-ROC = 0.912} vs. \textbf{Protocol 1} F1 = 0.612). Operationalising the DeLone \& McLean IS Success Model, \textbf{Protocol 2} reduces inspectorate search space by 92.6\% and translates neural loss into Explainable AI (XAI) audit checklists for APIP field inspections.

\keywords{Village Fund Governance \and Unsupervised Anomaly Detection \and Design Science Research \and Dual-Path Consensus \and Explainable AI \and Corruption Typology.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Indonesia's \textit{Dana Desa} (Village Fund) programme represents one of the largest intergovernmental fiscal transfer initiatives in the developing world, channelling approximately Rp 71 trillion annually to 75,259 villages across the archipelago since the enactment of Law No.\ 6 of 2014 on Villages~\cite{ref1}. The fiscal scale of this decentralised transfer programme, however, has severely outpaced the supervisory infrastructure required to guarantee its financial integrity. By 2024, Indonesian Corruption Watch (ICW) documented 591 court verdicts involving village fund misappropriation, implicating 640 criminal defendants and verifying Rp 598.13 billion in direct state financial losses~\cite{ref2}. Concurrently, the Anti-Corruption Learning Center of the Corruption Eradication Commission (ACLC KPK) recorded 851 village fund corruption cases implicating 973 suspects among village heads (\textit{Kepala Desa}) and rural apparatus across the 2015--2022 transfer cycle (absorbing Rp 468.9 trillion)~\cite{ref3}. These empirical statistics demonstrate that traditional institutional responses remain fundamentally reactive, depending on legal prosecution initiated two to five years after fund diversion has occurred, rather than proactive, automated detection embedded directly within active disbursement cycles.

The technical infrastructure for proactive screening already exists within latent administrative databases. The \textit{Sistem Keuangan Desa} (Siskeudes), developed and maintained by BPKP and the Ministry of Home Affairs (\textit{Kemendagri}), records expenditure transactions at the activity level across every stage of fiscal absorption: what specific output activity was planned, how much budget ceiling was allocated (\textit{Pagu}), how much expenditure was realised across sequential tranches (T1, T2, T3), and through what procurement mechanism (\textit{Cara Pengadaan}). This granular absorption dataset constitutes an administrative signal archive that latently encodes the statistical signatures distinguishing legitimate municipal execution from fraudulent diversion. Nevertheless, no systematic, automated screening artifact currently operates at the district or provincial level to interrogate this archive before inspection windows close and final disbursements complete.

Existing literature on rural financial oversight concentrates predominantly on retrospective forensic auditing~\cite{ref4}, qualitative accountability frameworks~\cite{ref5}, and classical fraud triangle diagnostics~\cite{ref6}. Where machine learning enters public sector fraud detection, studies rely almost exclusively on supervised classification algorithms---a paradigm that fails in real-time administrative monitoring because pre-existing, ground-truth fraud labels are non-existent in active Siskeudes databases~\cite{ref13,ref14}. The present study addresses this operational chasm by adopting a **Design Science Research (DSR)** framework~\cite{ref5,ref10}. It designs, constructs, and evaluates a Comparative Research Framework evaluating a baseline majority voting artifact (\textbf{Protocol 1}) against an unsupervised Dual-Path Consensus Machine Learning artifact (\textbf{Protocol 2}) that detects expenditure anomalies across a longitudinal panel of 96,778 activity-level records (1,363 villages across 3 fiscal years, 2023--2025) in Jambi Province, operating entirely without pre-existing fraud labels.

\textbf{Jambi Province provides a structurally significant research locus.} The \texttt{jaga.id} civil-society transparency portal, managed by KPK, logged only 11 public complaint submissions attributed to Jambi Province---accounting for a mere 1.4\% of 761 national reports---whereas neighbouring provinces of comparable administrative scale registered significantly higher complaint volumes~\cite{ref26}. A low volume of voluntary public reporting does not signal low corruption exposure; rather, it reflects a low-monitoring environment. Alfada's panel GMM threshold analysis across 19 Indonesian provinces confirms that regions combining high intergovernmental transfer dependence with weak external monitoring exhibit systematically elevated corruption incidence---a structural profile directly matching Jambi Province~\cite{ref27}. Furthermore, district-level \textit{Aparat Pengawasan Intern Pemerintah} (APIP) inspectorates face severe operational capacity constraints: Srirejeki and Faturokhman~\cite{ref12} demonstrate that kabupaten APIP offices lack sufficient staffing (typically only 5 to 15 auditors per kabupaten) to manually inspect thousands of active village expenditure entries, generating a substantial gap between statutory monitoring mandates under Government Regulation No. 60/2008~\cite{ref33} and actual physical field coverage.

Contemporaneous judicial verdicts across Jambi Province confirm that legal prosecution lags range from two to five years behind fund execution:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Kasus Muara Hemat (Kerinci)}: Verified state loss of Rp 644 million, with an enforcement lag of 5 years~\cite{ref28,ref30}.
    \item \textit{Kasus Jambi Tulo (Muaro Jambi)}: Complete village fund freezing due to fictitious project indications exceeding Rp 300 million~\cite{ref29}.
    \item \textit{Kasus Mantan Kades Kerinci}: Misappropriation of physical construction funds amounting to Rp 644 million~\cite{ref30}.
    \item \textit{Kasus Pangkal Duri (Tanjung Jabung Timur)}: State financial loss reaching Rp 415 million~\cite{ref31}.
\end{enumerate}
All four judicial cases involved financial irregularities that generated quantifiable signals in Siskeudes absorption records: fictitious construction outputs, zero-realisation procurement activities, and uncompetitive Swakelola budget execution---precisely the patterns targeted by the feature engineering and Dual-Path consensus architecture in this study.

This study delivers three primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Methodological Contribution (Comparative Research Framework)}: It constructs an unsupervised Dual-Path Consensus Machine Learning framework combining local density estimation (LOF) and global anomaly convergence (IF $\cap$ RDA) with an Operational Policy Mapping Layer, resolving the mutual cancellation limitation of traditional majority voting (\textbf{Protocol 1}).
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Contribution}: It operationalises Agency Theory, the Fraud Diamond, and Principal-Agent information asymmetry within administrative Siskeudes records, bridging Information Systems (IS) governance literature and computational fraud detection.
    \item \textbf{Practical Contribution}: It provides an ex-ante evaluation framework that reduces APIP inspection search space by 92.6\% at the activity level and prioritises 702 persistent high-risk villages (Tier 1), generating instance-level Explainable AI (XAI) audit checklists for field inspectors.
\end{enumerate}

This study addresses three core Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: Which feature constructs derived from Siskeudes expenditure absorption data serve as the most discriminating signals of expenditure anomaly?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: Which algorithmic paradigm---Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor, or Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder---and ensemble gating mechanism demonstrates superior anomaly discrimination on longitudinal village fund data?
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: How do algorithmically identified anomalies map to established corruption typologies, and how can XAI loss decomposition translate mathematical outliers into actionable APIP inspection checklists?
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work \& Conceptual Framework}

\subsection{Corruption Typology Frameworks}
Understanding the target of unsupervised detection requires a precise, domain-grounded taxonomy of corruption forms. Bussell~\cite{ref4} proposes a pragmatic two-dimensional typology distinguishing corruption by access type (monetary vs. preferential) and governance level (bureaucratic vs. political). Graycar's TASP framework---Types, Activities, Sectors, Places---extends this by asserting that anti-corruption mechanisms must specify not merely what form of corruption occurs, but within which specific public programme activity and geographic locus it manifests~\cite{ref7}.

For Indonesia's \textit{Dana Desa} context, Siregar and Aminudin~\cite{ref13} provide the primary empirical classification: a multi-case study identifying five recurring modus operandi---unit price mark-up of goods and services, fictitious budget items (\textit{proyek fiktif}), double budgeting, procurement manipulation, and misuse of personnel honoraria. Kartadinata et al.~\cite{ref14} extend this taxonomy through systematic analysis of over 200 KPK prosecution cases, confirming mark-up (\textit{penggelembungan}) and fictitious projects as the dominant physical corruption moduses. Recent empirical work by Medan et al.~\cite{ref15} documents that these exact modus operandi patterns persist into contemporary fiscal periods, validating typology-grounded detection rules.

\subsection{Fraud Diamond and Principal-Agent Foundations}
The Fraud Triangle (Cressey, 1953)~\cite{ref17}, operationalised in rural governance by Hidajat~\cite{ref6}, models fraud via three conditions: Pressure, Opportunity, and Rationalisation. Wolfe and Hermanson's Fraud Diamond extends this model by incorporating \textbf{Capability}---the individual authority, digital access, and technical competence required to execute and conceal financial manipulation~\cite{ref17b}. In Siskeudes execution, Capability resides in the Village Head (\textit{Kepala Desa}) and Financial Officer (\textit{Kaur Keuangan}), who maintain exclusive digital authorization credentials and bank withdrawal specimen signatures.

Opportunity is structurally driven by procurement method selection. Dataset audit reveals that \textbf{98.8\% of all village fund activities} in Jambi Province are executed through self-managed procurement (\textit{Swakelola}), completely bypassing open competitive bidding. This empirical dominance confirms S{\o}reide's~\cite{ref9} procurement corruption theory: the structural absence of market competition removes natural price-discovery mechanisms, enabling local officials to select favored vendors or inflate invoices without external challenge.

Principal-Agent Theory~\cite{ref25} frames the village head as an Agent possessing severe information asymmetry relative to the Principal (district APIP inspectorate, BPKP, KPK):
\begin{equation}
\text{InfoAsymmetry} = \mathcal{I}_{\text{Agent}}(\text{PhysicalRealization}, \text{TrueCost}) - \mathcal{I}_{\text{Principal}}(\text{SiskeudesReports})
\end{equation}
Unexplained statistical deviations in unit costs, tranche disbursement progress ratios, and procurement categories constitute proximate empirical traces of moral hazard and information asymmetry exploitation~\cite{ref9,ref25}.

\subsection{Anomaly Detection Paradigms in Public Financial Data}
The anomaly detection literature applied to public financial management has converged on three methodologically distinct paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Sparsity Partitioning}: Isolation Forest (Liu et al.~\cite{ref18,ref19}) measures random partitioning path length in tree ensembles, performing optimally against globally extreme multi-feature outliers.
    \item \textbf{Local Density Ratio Estimation}: Local Outlier Factor (LOF, Breunig et al.~\cite{ref24}) evaluates local reachability density ratios relative to $k$-nearest neighbors, isolating records that are locally sparse relative to their specific activity peer group (e.g., within a specific output code \texttt{Kode\_Output}).
    \item \textbf{Neural Reconstruction Error}: Deep Autoencoders (Zhou \& Paffenroth~\cite{ref32}) learn compressed latent representations of normal financial behaviour. Records exhibiting anomalously high Mean Squared Error (MSE) at reconstruction signal non-linear feature interaction distortions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Geographical Baseline Centering \& Engineered Feature Matrix}
Seven core feature constructs were engineered to operationalise documented corruption modus operandi:
\begin{equation}
z_{i,c,k,t} = \frac{x_{i,c,k,t} - \mu_{c,k,t}}{\sigma_{c,k,t} + \epsilon} \quad \text{within } (c, k, t) = (\text{Kode\_Output}, \text{Kabupaten\_Kota}, \text{Tahun})

To prevent remote highland jurisdictions (e.g., Kabupaten Kerinci) from generating systematic false-positive flags due to elevated baseline transportation costs, \texttt{cost\_deviation\_by\_category} calculates year-stratified z-scores strictly within the combined grouping $(c, k, t)$. This controls for regional logistics overhead while preserving sensitivity to local price manipulation.

All continuous features were scaled using \textbf{RobustScaler} (median centring, IQR scaling), resisting distortion by extreme outlier records.

\subsection{Algorithmic Paradigm Specifications}

\subsubsection{1. Isolation Forest (Global Sparsity Engine)}
Isolation Forest partitions the feature space via recursive random splits~\cite{ref18}. Path length $h(x)$ across $T=200$ trees determines anomaly score $s(x, n) = 2^{-\mathbb{E}(h(x))/c(n)}$. Contamination $c = 0.10$ sets the decision function at the 90th percentile:
\begin{equation}
\text{IF-Flag}_i = \mathbf{1}\left(s(x_i, n) \ge \text{Quantile}_{0.90}(s)\right) \implies N_{\text{IF}} = 9,678 \text{ records (10.00\%)}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{2. Local Outlier Factor (Density Ratio Engine)}
LOF computes the local reachability density ($\text{lrd}_k$) ratio between instance $p$ and its $k=20$ nearest neighbors~\cite{ref24}:
\begin{equation}
\text{LOF}_k(p) = \frac{1}{|N_k(p)|} \sum_{o \in N_k(p)} \frac{\text{lrd}_k(o)}{\text{lrd}_k(p)}
\end{equation}
Flagging threshold is set at the 95th percentile ($N_{\text{LOF}} = 4,839$ records / 5.00\%).

\subsubsection{3. Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder (RDA \& Neural Loss Attribution)}
The RDA network features an 8-layer symmetric bottleneck structure \texttt{[27 -> 64 -> 32 -> 16 -> 8 -> 16 -> 32 -> 64 -> 27]} trained with MSE loss and $L_2$ weight regularization ($\lambda = 1\times 10^{-3}$). Reconstruction error $E_i$ and per-feature contribution $e_{i,f}$ are computed as:
\begin{equation}
E_i = \sum_{f=1}^{d} (x_{i,f} - \hat{x}_{i,f})^2, \quad e_{i,f} = \frac{(x_{i,f} - \hat{x}_{i,f})^2}{E_i}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{RDA-Flag}_i = \mathbf{1}\left(E_i \ge \text{Quantile}_{0.95}(E)\right) \implies N_{\text{RDA}} = 4,840 \text{ records (5.00\%)}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dual-Path Consensus Ensemble Gate}
In baseline \textbf{Protocol 1}, simple majority voting ($\sum \text{Flag}_m \ge 2$) suffered from severe mutual cancellation because algorithms project onto orthogonal statistical subspaces. In \textbf{Protocol 2}, the \textbf{Dual-Path Consensus Ensemble Gate} explicitly decouples local density detection from multi-model global convergence:
\begin{equation}
\text{Consensus-Flag}_i = \text{LOF-Flag}_i \lor \left(\text{IF-Flag}_i \land \text{RDA-Flag}_i\right)
\end{equation}
This dual-path gate admits \textbf{7,153 consensus anomalous records (7.39\%)}, capturing 3,940 local density isolates (LOF only) and 2,314 global multi-model convergence records (IF $\cap$ RDA) without admitting uncoordinated single-model noise.

\subsection{Algorithmic Pseudocode Specifications and Execution Dynamics}

The operational execution of the proposed Design Science Research (DSR) artifact proceeds sequentially across three formal pseudocode algorithms. This multi-stage pipeline translates raw administrative Siskeudes expenditure absorption entries into verified, domain-grounded audit intelligence, addressing the ground-truth paradox through data hygiene, multi-paradigm unsupervised detection, and Explainable AI (XAI) operational mapping.

\subsubsection{Stage 1: Data Hygiene and Stratified Regional Centering}
Raw village fund expenditure records cannot be ingested directly into machine learning models due to administrative recording noise, zero-volume entry errors ($\text{Volume}_i \le 0$), invalid output activity codes, and severe regional cost heterogeneity. Baseline unit costs ($\text{Realization\_Total}_i / \text{Volume}_i$) exhibit substantial variance across geographic regions due to local transportation overheads, terrain difficulty, and regional market disparities (e.g., remote highland jurisdictions such as Kabupaten Kerinci versus lowland urban centers). Direct global normalization without regional stratification introduces systematic false positives for isolated jurisdictions. 

To eliminate baseline inflation skew, Algorithm~\ref{alg:baseline_centering} executes a two-phase data hygiene and stratification protocol. First, invalid zero-volume records and corrupt output activity codes are purged. Second, continuous cost variables are centered and scaled by calculating year-stratified regional z-scores $z_{i, \text{cat}}$ strictly within each distinct subgroup $(c, k, t) = (\text{Kode\_Output}, \text{Kabupaten\_Kota}, \text{Tahun})$. This controls for local logistical overhead while preserving sensitivity to genuine within-jurisdiction price inflation. Finally, non-parametric RobustScaler transformation (median centering, interquartile range scaling) is applied across continuous columns to prevent extreme outlier values from distorting scale bounds.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Year-Stratified Regional Baseline Centering and Scaling}
\label{alg:baseline_centering}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Raw Dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\}$, Category $c_i$, Jurisdiction $k_i$, Year $t_i$.
\ENSURE Scaled Feature Matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$.
\STATE Filter invalid records: $\mathcal{D}_{\text{clean}} \leftarrow \{x_i \in \mathcal{D} \mid \text{Volume}_i > 0 \land \text{ValidCode}(c_i)\}$.
\FOR{each unique stratum $(c, k, t)$}
    \STATE Extract subgroup records $\mathcal{S}_{c,k,t} \leftarrow \{x_i \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{clean}} \mid c_i = c \land k_i = k \land t_i = t\}$.
    \STATE Compute mean unit cost $\mu_{c,k,t}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{c,k,t}$.
    \FOR{each record $x_i \in \mathcal{S}_{c,k,t}$}
        \STATE $z_{i, \text{cat}} \leftarrow (\text{CostPerUnit}(x_i) - \mu_{c,k,t}) / (\sigma_{c,k,t} + \epsilon)$.
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Apply RobustScaler to continuous columns: $\mathbf{x}_i \leftarrow (\mathbf{x}_i - \text{Median}(\mathbf{X})) / \text{IQR}(\mathbf{X})$.
\RETURN Feature Matrix $\mathbf{X}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Stage 2: Multi-Paradigm Unsupervised Detection and Dual-Path Gating}
The output of Stage 1 is a standardized, 27-variable feature matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ representing $N=96,778$ cleaned activity entries. Because individual unsupervised detection paradigms project onto orthogonal statistical subspaces, single-model detectors exhibit distinct diagnostic blind spots when operating independently. Isolation Forest (Path A) measures random decision tree partitioning path lengths, excelling at global multi-feature outliers but missing localized density isolates. Local Outlier Factor (Path B) computes local reachability density ratios relative to $k=20$ nearest activity peers, isolating localized density anomalies such as zero-progress ghost projects within small output categories. Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder (Path C) trains an 8-layer bottleneck neural network to learn non-linear feature correlations, flagging transactions with high reconstruction Mean Squared Error (MSE).

In baseline \textbf{Protocol 1}, simple majority voting ($\sum \text{Flag}_m \ge 2$) suffered from severe mutual cancellation because demanding simultaneous consensus across all three orthogonal paradigms discarded 3,940 localized density isolates flagged exclusively by LOF. To resolve this architectural limitation, Algorithm~\ref{alg:dual_path_ensemble} executes parallel model training across Paths A, B, and C, and applies the \textbf{Dual-Path Consensus Gate} ($\text{LOF} \lor (\text{IF} \land \text{RDA})$). This gating logic decouples local density isolation from multi-model global convergence, synthesizing raw model scores into a robust binary consensus flag vector $\mathbf{y}_{\text{consensus}} \in \{0, 1\}^N$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Dual-Path Consensus Ensemble Detection Gate}
\label{alg:dual_path_ensemble}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Feature Matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, IF contamination $c = 0.10$, LOF $k = 20$, RDA bottleneck $h = 8$, percentile $q = 0.95$.
\ENSURE Consensus Flag Vector $\mathbf{y}_{\text{consensus}} \in \{0, 1\}^N$.
\STATE \textbf{Path A (Global Sparsity):} Fit Isolation Forest $\mathcal{M}_{\text{IF}}(\mathbf{X})$ with $T=200$ trees.
\FOR{$i = 1 \dots N$}
    \STATE $y_{i, \text{IF}} \leftarrow \mathbf{1}\left(s_{\text{IF}}(x_i) \ge \text{Quantile}_{0.90}(s_{\text{IF}})\right)$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Path B (Local Density Ratio):} Compute LOF scores $\text{LOF}_k(x_i)$.
\FOR{$i = 1 \dots N$}
    \STATE $y_{i, \text{LOF}} \leftarrow \mathbf{1}\left(\text{LOF}_k(x_i) \ge \text{Quantile}_{0.95}(\text{LOF})\right)$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Path C (Neural Reconstruction):} Train Autoencoder $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i = g_{\phi}(f_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i))$ via MSE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}$.
\FOR{$i = 1 \dots N$}
    \STATE $E_i \leftarrow \|\mathbf{x}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\|_2^2$; \quad $y_{i, \text{RDA}} \leftarrow \mathbf{1}\left(E_i \ge \text{Quantile}_{0.95}(E)\right)$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Dual-Path Consensus Gating Logic:}
\FOR{$i = 1 \dots N$}
    \STATE $y_{i, \text{consensus}} \leftarrow y_{i, \text{LOF}} \lor \left(y_{i, \text{IF}} \land y_{i, \text{RDA}}\right)$.
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\mathbf{y}_{\text{consensus}}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Stage 3: Operational Policy Typology Mapping and XAI Loss Attribution}
While Algorithm~\ref{alg:dual_path_ensemble} identifies $7,153$ consensus anomalous activities (7.39\% of total absorption), raw mathematical outlier flags are insufficient for field deployment because public sector inspectors require domain-interpretable corruption typologies and feature-level diagnostic evidence. Algorithm~\ref{alg:typology_xai} bridges the gap between machine learning predictions and formal administrative auditing through a two-step translation process.

First, Algorithm~\ref{alg:typology_xai} evaluates domain-grounded conditional business rules to map consensus-flagged transactions into seven specific corruption typologies ($T_1 \dots T_7$), such as Unit Price Mark-Up ($T_1$), Ghost Activity ($T_2$), Procurement Irregularity ($T_5$), and Cross-Category Activity Dumping ($T_7$). Transactions that exhibit joint multi-variable shifts without triggering individual single-variable heuristic limits are mapped to the \textit{Unclassified Subthreshold Risk} category ($N=1,227$). Second, the algorithm decomposes the neural RDA reconstruction loss $E_i = \|\mathbf{x}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\|_2^2$ into relative per-feature error contributions $e_{i,f} = (x_{i,f} - \hat{x}_{i,f})^2 / E_i$. Sorting features by $e_{i,f}$ in descending order generates instance-level Explainable AI (XAI) audit checklists $\mathcal{C}_i$, providing district APIP inspectors with specific documentary verification targets for field investigations.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Operational Policy Typology Mapping and XAI Loss Attribution}
\label{alg:typology_xai}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Consensus Set $\mathcal{A} = \{x_i \mid y_{i, \text{consensus}} = 1\}$, Errors $\{E_i\}$, Matrix $\mathbf{X}$.
\ENSURE Typologies $\mathcal{T}_i \subseteq \{T_1, \dots, T_7\}$ and XAI Audit Checklists $\mathcal{C}_i$.
\FOR{each flagged instance $x_i \in \mathcal{A}$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \emptyset$.
    \IF{$z_{i, \text{unit\_cost}} > 3.0 \sigma$} \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \mathcal{T}_i \cup \{T_1 \text{ (Mark-Up)}\}$. \ENDIF
    \IF{$\text{AbsorptionRatio}(x_i) < 0.05 \land \text{AvgCompletion}(x_i) < 0.10$} \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \mathcal{T}_i \cup \{T_2 \text{ (Ghost Activity)}\}$. \ENDIF
    \IF{$\text{SwakelolaHighValue}(x_i) = 1 \land \text{CostPerUnit}(x_i) > Q_{0.75}$} \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \mathcal{T}_i \cup \{T_5 \text{ (Procurement Irregularity)}\}$. \ENDIF
    \IF{$z_{i, \text{cat}} > 3.0 \sigma$} \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \mathcal{T}_i \cup \{T_7 \text{ (Cross-Category Dumping)}\}$. \ENDIF
    \IF{$\mathcal{T}_i = \emptyset$} \STATE $\mathcal{T}_i \leftarrow \{\text{Unclassified Subthreshold Risk}\}$. \ENDIF
    \FOR{$f = 1 \dots d$}
        \STATE $e_{i,f} \leftarrow (x_{i,f} - \hat{x}_{i,f})^2 / E_i$.
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Rank features by $e_{i,f}$ descending to construct audit checklist $\mathcal{C}_i$.
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{\mathcal{T}_i\}_{x_i \in \mathcal{A}}$ and $\{\mathcal{C}_i\}_{x_i \in \mathcal{A}}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The resulting output tuples $(\mathcal{T}_i, \mathcal{C}_i)$ generated by Algorithm~\ref{alg:typology_xai} provide the empirical foundation for the operational screening results presented in Section 4 and the legal indication framework detailed in Section 5.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Principal Empirical Findings \& Artifact Evolution}
This study developed and evaluated \textbf{Protocol 2}, an unsupervised Dual-Path Machine Learning artifact for detecting expenditure anomalies across 96,778 activity entries (2023--2025) in Jambi Province. Three core empirical insights emerge from the evaluation.

First, the study resolves the operationalization chasm between qualitative governance research and non-communicating computational models. Protocol 2 ingests decentralized administrative Siskeudes absorption data from \texttt{jaga.id}~\cite{ref26}, proving that administrative absorption signals contain quantifiable traces of expenditure distortion without requiring pre-existing fraud labels.

Second, Protocol 2 overcomes the Ground Truth Paradox via Dual-Path Gating ($\text{LOF} \lor (\text{IF} \land \text{RDA})$), expanding anomaly recall from 3,107 to 7,153 records (7.39\%). Ex-ante benchmark evaluation confirms superior anomaly recovery (\textbf{Precision = 0.846, Recall = 0.846, F1 = 0.846, AUC-ROC = 0.912}), outperforming single-algorithm baselines.

Third, implementing kabupaten-stratified z-score centering ($(c,k,t)$) eliminated systematic false positives for isolated highland jurisdictions (e.g., Kabupaten Kerinci), reducing Kerinci false flags from 31.0\% to 4.2\% while preserving sensitivity to genuine local price manipulation.

\subsection{Algorithmic Effectiveness and Operational Screening Performance}
The empirical results establish the multi-dimensional effectiveness of \textbf{Protocol 2} across four primary operational dimensions:

\subsubsection{1. Resolution of Mutual Cancellation in Multi-Detector Ensembles}
Traditional majority voting (\textbf{Protocol 1}: $\text{IF} \land \text{LOF} \land \text{RDA}$) suffered from severe mutual cancellation because Local Outlier Factor (LOF) measures local reachability density ratios, whereas Isolation Forest (IF) and Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder (RDA) evaluate global feature space partitioning. Demanding simultaneous consensus across all three algorithms discarded localized density anomalies (such as zero-progress ghost projects occurring within small output categories), capping recall at 3,107 records (3.12\%). \textbf{Protocol 2} resolves this conflict through Dual-Path Gating ($\text{LOF} \lor (\text{IF} \land \text{RDA})$), which decouples local density isolates from multi-model global convergence. This architectural innovation expands recall to \textbf{7,153 consensus anomalous activities (7.39\% of total provincial absorption)}, capturing \textbf{Rp 642.85 billion} in financial realization risk (14.89\% of total provincial disbursement) and \textbf{Rp 6.08 trillion} in budget ceiling exposure (7.46\%).

\subsubsection{2. Ex-Ante Synthetic Benchmark Superiority}
Evaluating \textbf{Protocol 2} on a controlled synthetic fraud benchmark ($N=10,000$, 500 injected fraud cases / 5.0\% prevalence) validates its superior recovery performance. \textbf{Protocol 2} achieves \textbf{Precision = 0.846, Recall = 0.846, F1-Score = 0.846, and AUC-ROC = 0.912}, outperforming individual base detectors---Isolation Forest ($\text{F1} = 0.724$), Local Outlier Factor ($\text{F1} = 0.686$), and Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder ($\text{F1} = 0.812$)---as well as Protocol 1 majority voting ($\text{F1} = 0.568$, $\text{Recall} = 0.510$). This demonstrates that combining non-linear neural autoencoders with local density estimators delivers robust anomaly boundary separation under heavy-tailed noise.

\subsubsection{3. Audit Search Space Optimization and Target Prioritization}
From an operational oversight standpoint, effectiveness is defined by screening efficiency. \textbf{Protocol 2} achieves a \textbf{92.6\% reduction} in audit search space, filtering 96,778 activity entries down to 7,153 high-priority targets. Given that municipal inspectorates (\textit{APIP}) typically operate with only 5 to 15 auditors per kabupaten~\cite{ref12}, manual review of 96k records is physically impossible. Furthermore, longitudinal persistence tracking across the 3-year panel isolates \textbf{702 Tier-1 persistent high-risk villages (51.5\% of tracked jurisdictions)} flagged continuously from 2023 to 2025 ($P_v = 1.0$). This provides APIP leadership with a defensible, risk-ranked inspection schedule.

\subsubsection{4. Sub-Threshold Sensitivity and Compound Risk Capture}
\textbf{Protocol 2} demonstrates unique effectiveness in detecting sub-threshold financial manipulation. Fraud perpetrators frequently calibrate expenditure anomalies just below single-variable heuristic thresholds (e.g., setting unit cost deviation at $+1.8\sigma$ versus a $+2.0\sigma$ flag limit). Single-rule policy engines fail to detect these calibrated distortions. In contrast, the joint non-linear feature space evaluated by LOF and RDA exposes joint multi-variable shifts, successfully capturing \textbf{1,227 sub-threshold masked activities} accounting for \textbf{Rp 125.07 billion} in latent financial risk.

\subsection{Diagnostic Root-Cause Analysis of Anomaly Typology Shifts}
To understand the structural mechanisms driving the primary typology shifts between \textbf{Protocol 1} and \textbf{Protocol 2}, systematic root-cause diagnostic inquiries were conducted and synthesized into cohesive analytical narratives:

\subsubsection{1. Causal Mechanics of T2 Ghost Activity Explosion ($N=4,155$)}
The dramatic surge in T2 Ghost Activity flags---jumping from 774 records (24.9\%) in Protocol 1 to 4,155 records (58.1\%) in Protocol 2---stems from a fundamental diagnostic capability shift. Protocol 2 successfully isolates thousands of expenditure activities where budget funds were fully drawn down across tranches while physical output completion remained near zero. In baseline Protocol 1, these zero-progress activities were systematically missed because majority voting relied heavily on Isolation Forest's global feature path lengths. Because zero physical progress occurring within small output categories creates localized clusters, these entries appeared globally normal to random decision tree splits. Protocol 2 overcomes this diagnostic blindness through Local Outlier Factor (LOF), which evaluates local reachability density ratios relative to adjacent peer villages that completed physical infrastructure under identical output codes. By exposing zero-progress activities as extreme local density isolates, Protocol 2 targets ghost projects (\textit{proyek fiktif})---the most severe physical fraud modus in Indonesian rural administration, accounting for Rp 181.22 billion in direct financial realization risk.

\subsubsection{2. Causal Mechanics of T5 Procurement Irregularity Surge ($N=2,343$)}
Similarly, the surge in T5 Procurement Irregularity flags---expanding from 26 records (0.8\%) in Protocol 1 to 2,343 records (32.8\%) in Protocol 2---reflects the neural autoencoder's capacity to capture non-linear feature interactions. Protocol 2 incorporates the \texttt{swakelola\_high\_value} indicator directly into the Reconstruction Dense Autoencoder (RDA) feature matrix. In contrast, Protocol 1 failed to isolate uncompetitive high-value Swakelola procurement because it enforced rigid linear rule combinations requiring simultaneous violations across multiple continuous cost metrics. In practice, procurement manipulation operates through subtle non-linear interactions between categorical procurement modes (Swakelola) and continuous unit cost distributions. The 8-layer bottleneck neural network in RDA learns these complex feature correlations during unsupervised training, producing elevated reconstruction Mean Squared Error (MSE) whenever high-value projects bypass competitive bidding. Capturing T5 irregularities is essential for governance reform because 98.8\% of village activities in Jambi Province rely on Swakelola; isolating uncompetitive high-value projects directly targets the structural vulnerability identified in S{\o}reide's procurement corruption theory~\cite{ref9}.

\subsection{Sub-Threshold Masking \& Unclassified Anomaly Subspace ($N=1,227$)}
Perpetrators intentionally manipulate multiple financial variables just below individual heuristic thresholds (e.g., unit cost deviation at $+1.8\sigma$ vs $+2.0\sigma$ threshold; completion delay at $-1.7\sigma$ vs $-2.0\sigma$). Multi-dimensional ML models (LOF and RDA) detect joint probability distance across features, correctly flagging the activity while heuristic policy mapping leaves it Unclassified, capturing \textbf{Rp 125.07 billion} in sub-threshold financial risk.

Jaccard overlap matrix analysis on the Top-50 anomalies reveals high subspace independence: IF vs LOF (1.01\% overlap), IF vs RDA (0.00\% overlap), LOF vs RDA (3.09\% overlap), and Consensus vs RDA (96.08\% capture rate).

\subsection{Legal Indications and Evidentiary Boundaries for Public Sector Audits}
Operationalizing machine learning within public sector corruption oversight requires strict alignment with administrative law and criminal procedure boundaries. The deployment of \textbf{Protocol 2} carries three critical legal implications:

\subsubsection{1. Statutory Status as Early Audit Indication and Investigative Support (\textit{Indikasi Awal dan Dukungan Penyelidikan})} Machine learning anomaly scores generated by \textbf{Protocol 2} do \textbf{not} constitute direct statutory evidence (\textit{alat bukti yang sah}) under Article 184 of the Indonesian Criminal Procedure Code (KUHAP / Law No. 8/1981)~\cite{ref35} or the Corruption Eradication Act (Law No. 31/1999 jo. Law No. 20/2001)~\cite{ref34}. Under Indonesian criminal procedure jurisprudence (KUHAP Art. 1 point 5 jo. Constitutional Court Decision No. 21/PUU-XII/2014), statutory preliminary evidence (\textit{bukti permulaan yang cukup}) must be independently established by law enforcement inquirers (\textit{penyelidik}) during the preliminary inquiry stage (\textit{penyelidikan}) to elevate a case to a full criminal investigation (\textit{penyidikan}). Algorithmic outputs cannot substitute for \textit{bukti permulaan yang cukup}, nor do they qualify as statutory presumption evidence (\textit{alat bukti petunjuk}, KUHAP Art. 188). Instead, AI analytical findings function strictly as \textbf{operational investigative leads} (\textit{indikasi awal / petunjuk operasional}) supporting the \textit{pro-justitia} inquiry process. To establish legal standing and substantiate formal preliminary evidence, algorithmic findings mandate corroboration and methodological justification by certified Expert Witnesses (\textit{Keterangan Ahli}, KUHAP Art. 184(1)b jo. Art. 186) alongside formal forensic audit documentation.
Beyond criminal enforcement (\textit{penindakan}), the primary statutory value of \textbf{Protocol 2} lies in corruption prevention (\textit{pencegahan tindak pidana korupsi}) under Government Regulation (\textit{Peraturan Pemerintah}) No. 60/2008 on the Government Internal Control System (\textit{SPIP})~\cite{ref33}. Algorithmic anomaly detection provides public sector oversight bodies---\textit{Aparat Pengawasan Intern Pemerintah} (APIP), the Supreme Audit Agency (\textit{BPK}), and the Financial and Development Supervisory Board (\textit{BPKP})---with an objective, data-driven mechanism to uncover early fraud patterns and close systemic governance loopholes before authorized actors across Regional Government Apparatus (\textit{Organisasi Perangkat Daerah} -- OPD) and Village Government Apparatus (\textit{Perangkat Desa}) can exploit institutional vulnerabilities.

\subsubsection{2. Differentiating Administrative Irregularity from Criminal Intent (\textit{Mens Rea})}
A fundamental boundary in public sector anomaly detection is distinguishing non-malicious operational failure from criminal corruption. A high reconstruction MSE or density outlier score may reflect non-corrupt causes: severe rural administrative capacity deficits, delayed milestone entry by village secretaries (\textit{Sekretaris Desa}), emergency procurement during local natural disasters, or unrecorded localized material cost inflation. To establish criminal corruption under Law No. 31/1999 Art. 2 \& 3~\cite{ref34}, legal authorities must prove two distinct elements: (a) an unlawful act or abuse of official authority (\textit{perbuatan melawan hukum / menyalahgunakan kewenangan}), and (b) intentional state financial loss (\textit{merugikan keuangan negara}) or self/corporate enrichment. Algorithmic outputs measure statistical deviation, not legal culpability; they direct human auditors to transactions where the probability of underlying unlawful behavior is statistically concentrated.

\subsubsection{3. XAI-to-Evidence Chain of Custody for APIP Field Inspections}
To translate mathematical anomaly metrics into legally admissible audit evidence, \textbf{Protocol 2} incorporates Explainable AI (XAI) feature error decomposition. Standard "black-box" machine learning predictions are inadmissible in formal administrative disciplinary hearings or judicial proceedings. By decomposing the neural RDA reconstruction loss into per-feature error contributions, \textbf{Protocol 2} constructs a transparent evidentiary chain of custody:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unit Cost Inflation Errors} ($\text{MSE}_{\text{cost}} > 0.04$) $\to$ APIP issue audit assignment orders (\textit{Surat Perintah Tugas Inspektorat}) to subpoena supplier invoices, receipt vouchers (\textit{Kwitansi}), and verify alignment with official regional price standards (\textit{Standar Satuan Harga -- SSH}).
    \item \textbf{Completion vs. Disbursement Mismatches} (T2 Ghost Activity) $\to$ APIP conduct physical field inspections (\textit{Cek Fisik Lapangan}) using GPS-tagged physical volume measurements combined with bank statement reconciliation (\textit{Rekening Kas Desa}).
    \item \textbf{Procurement Anomalies} (T5 Swakelola High-Value) $\to$ APIP examine procurement committee minutes (\textit{Berita Acara Pengadaan}) and Activity Management Team (\textit{TPK}) authorization logs.
\end{enumerate}
By anchoring algorithmic alerts to specific documentary and physical verification protocols, \textbf{Protocol 2} bridges the gap between unsupervised machine learning and formal forensic audit standards.

\subsection{Threats to Validity, Methodological Limitations, and Future Directions}
While \textbf{Protocol 2} delivers substantial gains in anomaly recall, search space reduction, and longitudinal target prioritization, several methodological threats to validity and operational limitations must be acknowledged:

\subsubsection{1. Absence of In-Situ Ground-Truth Labels \& Synthetic Benchmark Reliance}
A primary methodological boundary in public sector anomaly detection is the \textit{Ground-Truth Paradox}: active administrative Siskeudes databases lack pre-labeled, audited fraud tags. Consequently, ex-ante model validation relies on a controlled synthetic fraud benchmark ($N=10,000$, 500 injected fraud cases / 5.0\% prevalence) where \textbf{Protocol 2} achieves $\text{F1}=0.846$ and $\text{AUC-ROC}=0.912$. While synthetic injection validates architectural recovery performance against known distortion distributions, synthetic benchmarks cannot fully capture all complex, evolving real-world collusion tactics. Therefore, algorithmic flags must be treated strictly as statutory preliminary audit indications (\textit{indikasi awal}) requiring human forensic verification via APIP field inspections (\textit{Audit Tujuan Tertentu}).

\subsubsection{2. Dependence on Administrative Siskeudes Signal Quality \& Off-Book Collusion}
The artifact's diagnostic capability relies fundamentally on the administrative absorption signals recorded within the \texttt{jaga.id} / Siskeudes database. The model effectively isolates digital anomalies, including fictitious output reporting, tranche absorption delays, Swakelola price mark-ups, and cross-category expenditure dumping. However, the system is structurally blind to physical off-book corruption mechanisms---such as unrecorded cash kickbacks, direct vendor bribery outside bank accounts (\textit{Rekening Kas Desa}), or coercive extortion---that leave no latent statistical footprint within official administrative ledgers.

\subsubsection{3. Geographic Locus \& Regional Baseline Generalizability}
The empirical evaluation was conducted across 96,778 activity entries from 1,363 villages in Jambi Province (2023--2025). Although year-stratified regional z-score centering ($(c,k,t) = (\text{Kode\_Output}, \text{Kabupaten\_Kota}, \text{Tahun})$) successfully controls for local logistical cost variations between urban centers and isolated highland regencies (e.g., Kabupaten Kerinci), adapting the framework to other Indonesian provinces (such as Papua, East Nusa Tenggara, or Java) requires recalibrating regional price baselines and categorical output structures to reflect regional economic disparities.

\subsubsection{4. Static Operational Business Rules \& Unclassified Subthreshold Risk}
The Operational Policy Mapping Layer relies on deterministic business rule thresholds ($z_{i, \text{unit\_cost}} > 3.0\sigma$, $\text{AbsorptionRatio} < 0.05$, etc.), leaving 1,227 consensus-flagged activities (17.2\% of the flagged pool) categorized as \textit{Unclassified Subthreshold Risk}. These records represent compound multi-variable distortions where perpetrators intentionally calibrate individual feature shifts just below single-rule limits. Future research should integrate dynamic, fuzzy-logic policy engines or active learning feedback loops driven by APIP post-audit findings.

\section{Conclusion}

This study developed and evaluated an unsupervised Dual-Path Machine Learning artifact (\textbf{Protocol 2}), framed within Design Science Research (DSR)~\cite{ref10}, for detecting expenditure anomalies in village fund absorption records across 96,778 activity entries (1,363 villages, 2023--2025) in Jambi Province.

In response to \textbf{RQ1}, seven engineered feature constructs incorporating kabupaten-stratified z-score baseline centering (\texttt{cost\_deviation\_by\_category}) deliver superior discriminating power. Neural autoencoder loss decomposition confirms that within-category unit cost deviation and unit price inflation constitute the primary statistical drivers of financial distortion in Siskeudes records.

In response to \textbf{RQ2}, LOF isolates extreme heavy-tailed local density anomalies (BC = 0.957), while Isolation Forest (BC = 0.335) and Deep Autoencoder (BC = 0.703) partition global multi-feature outliers. The Dual-Path Consensus Ensemble ($\text{LOF} \lor (\text{IF} \land \text{RDA})$) resolves the mutual cancellation limitation of traditional majority voting, achieving \textbf{Precision = 0.846, Recall = 0.846, F1-Score = 0.846, and AUC-ROC = 0.912} on an ex-ante synthetic fraud benchmark.

In response to \textbf{RQ3}, an Operational Policy Mapping Layer translates mathematical outliers into seven corruption typologies dominated by \textbf{T2: Ghost Activities (58.1\% / 4,155 records)} and \textbf{T5: Procurement Irregularities (32.8\% / 2,343 records)}, isolating \textbf{Rp 642.85 billion} in realized expenditure risk. Instance-level XAI loss decomposition transforms neural MSE into actionable field inspection checklists for district APIP auditors.

Future research will extend this framework along four primary trajectories: implementing semi-supervised Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on transaction graphs to capture compound sub-threshold fraud networks; scaling the longitudinal pipeline to multi-province national panels; deploying streaming API microservices integrated with BPKP Siskeudes endpoints for real-time pre-disbursement alert generation; and conducting ex-post empirical field studies measuring APIP audit referral conversion rates and recovered state financial losses.
